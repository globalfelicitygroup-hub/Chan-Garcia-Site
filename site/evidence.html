Case 01 · Private Equity · Manufacturing Acquisition
PE Due Diligence Manufacturing Monterrey Illustrative

Standard legal due diligence was complete. The investment committee asked for an independent governance review before closing.

The situation

A U.S.-based private equity fund was evaluating the acquisition of a mid-size manufacturing company in Monterrey. Standard legal due diligence had been completed by external counsel. The investment committee required an independent governance and regulatory exposure review before approving the deal — specifically, a financially quantified view of what the identified risks would actually cost.

What we found

The assessment identified material exposure across three areas: labor compliance gaps under STPS regulations that the seller's counsel had characterized as administrative, a partially undocumented environmental permit history creating contingent liability, and a related-party transaction structure generating transfer pricing and tax contingency risk. Each finding was translated into a probability-weighted financial exposure range using actuarial methodology.

What changed

The fund used Chan García's quantified exposure report to renegotiate the purchase price, establish an escrow mechanism for potential remediation costs, and require specific representations and warranties addressing each identified risk. The deal closed on revised terms that reflected the actual financial exposure — not the seller's characterization of the risk.

Case 02 · U.S. Manufacturer · Nearshoring Entry
Market Entry Industrial Bajío Region Illustrative

Legal counsel had drafted the corporate structure. The CFO wanted an independent view of exposure before board approval.

The situation

A mid-size U.S. industrial company was evaluating a manufacturing subsidiary in the Bajío region to support nearshoring demand. Legal counsel had drafted the corporate structure and entity formation documents. The CFO wanted an independent assessment of governance and regulatory exposure before presenting the entry plan to the board for approval.

What we found

The review identified that the proposed structure underestimated IMMEX compliance obligations, created transfer pricing exposure given the intercompany arrangement, and lacked adequate governance documentation to satisfy the parent company's audit committee standards. Each gap was quantified as a financial exposure range with specific remediation cost estimates.

What changed

The board approved the Mexico entry on a revised corporate structure that addressed each identified exposure. A compliance remediation timeline was established, and the Year 1 governance budget was adjusted to reflect actual exposure rather than the original estimate. The CFO presented Chan García's exposure report directly to the audit committee as an independent validation of the entry decision.

"The entry happened. It happened with eyes open."
— CFO, U.S. Industrial Manufacturer

Case 03 · Family Office · Portfolio Monitoring
Ongoing Monitoring Multi-Sector Mexico City Illustrative

Quarterly governance monitoring across three operating companies surfaced risks a one-time review would have missed.

The situation

A Mexican family office with investments across three operating companies — a logistics operation, a real estate developer, and a financial services entity — engaged Chan García for quarterly governance risk monitoring across the portfolio. The objective was to establish an independent, recurring view of governance exposure that could be reported to the internal investment committee.

What we found

Over two quarterly monitoring cycles, the assessment identified a regulatory change materially increasing the financial services entity's SAT compliance exposure — a change that standard legal counsel had not flagged as financially material. The monitoring also identified deteriorating governance documentation in the logistics company ahead of a planned secondary offering, creating potential investor confidence risk.

What changed

Both issues were addressed before they became financial events. The logistics company's governance documentation was remediated in time for the offering. The financial services entity's SAT exposure was addressed proactively. The family office now uses Chan García's quarterly governance risk scores as a standard component of its internal investment committee reporting.

"Monitoring caught what a one-time review would have missed."
