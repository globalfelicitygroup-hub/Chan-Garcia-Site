Brand Identity System

Chan García

Visual identity guidelines for Chan García — independent regulatory risk and financial exposure advisory. This document defines the brand's color system, typography, logo usage, and visual direction.

Version 1.0 · March 2026

01 — Brand evolution

From legal practice to financial intelligence

The visual identity reflects the firm's evolution — from a traditional legal practice to an independent regulatory risk and financial exposure advisory serving institutional decision-makers.

Previous identity — what to retire
  • Heavy ornate serif that reads as traditional law firm
  • Metallic emboss treatment — does not translate to digital
  • "Abogados" as the primary descriptor anchors the brand in legal services
  • Decorative double-rule border frame
  • No scalable color system for web, print, and presentations
  • No connection to the institutional audience (PE, CFOs, investment committees)
New identity — defining elements
  • Clean serif with restrained weight signals precision
  • Copper horizontal rule as a signature brand element
  • "Regulatory Risk & Financial Exposure Advisors · Mexico" repositions the firm
  • Light, open palette with warm gray base — minimal contrast, maximum clarity
  • Typography-driven, no decorative elements
  • Single accent color (copper) creates a focused, confident visual identity
  • Reads as financial intelligence, not legal services

The updated identity is built on the principle that the visual brand should make institutional buyers — PE partners, CFOs, investment committee members — feel that this firm understands their world before they read a single word of copy.

02 — Color palette

White, warm gray, and copper

The palette is built on a light, clean foundation — white and warm grays that feel open, precise, and uncluttered. Copper is the sole accent color: warm, grounded, and distinctive. It functions as both the signature brand mark and the functional accent across all applications. This palette is calm, analytical, and distinctly not a law firm.

Semantic Colors

Exposure Red (#8B3A3A) — Used exclusively for quantified financial exposure values in case studies and reports. When a reader sees this color, it means "this is a financial number representing risk."

Outcome Green (#4A7A5C) — Used for decision outcomes and positive results. When a reader sees this color, it means "this is what changed because of the assessment." This is a functional color only — it does not appear in the brand palette outside of case study outcomes.

Why This Palette Works

The light, open base (white/snow/pearl) creates a sense of clarity and precision — the visual equivalent of a clean, well-organized assessment report. There's nothing hidden, nothing obscured. The brand breathes through white space.

Copper as the sole accent is the key decision. It's warmer and more grounded than gold — the material of precision instruments and architectural hardware, not fashion or luxury. It carries institutional weight without the associations that gold triggers (luxury, finance-bro, overused). Against the warm gray palette, copper feels inevitable — like it belongs there naturally.

The restraint of a single accent color is itself a brand statement. Two or three accent colors signal a firm trying to be multiple things. One accent — used consistently, used precisely — signals a firm that knows exactly what it is.

03 — Typography

Three typefaces, three roles

The type system is built around clarity of function. Each typeface has a specific role. The display face carries authority, the body face carries readability, and the mono face carries data and precision.

Display — Source Serif 4
We Make Governance Measurable.
Used for: Headlines, page titles, case study titles, pull quotes, tagline
Weights: 300 Light, 400 Regular, 500 Medium, 400 Italic
Character: A contemporary serif with moderate contrast — authoritative but approachable. Less ornate than traditional high-contrast serifs, which keeps it from feeling like a law firm. Excellent screen rendering at all sizes.
Body — Archivo
Chan García is an independent advisory firm that helps investment committees, private capital, and companies entering Mexico understand exactly what their legal, governance, and regulatory exposure costs — in financial terms, not legal abstractions.
Used for: Body text, descriptions, navigation, form labels, buttons
Weights: 200 ExtraLight, 300 Light, 400 Regular, 500 Medium, 600 SemiBold
Character: A grotesque sans-serif with a slightly condensed proportion — clean, modern, and highly readable. The condensed quality gives it a more distinctive personality than standard grotesques while maintaining excellent legibility at body sizes.
Data / Labels — JetBrains Mono
Regulatory Risk & Financial Exposure Advisors · Mexico
Used for: Section labels, categories, metadata, dates, credentials, data labels, descriptor lines
Weights: 300 Light, 400 Regular, 500 Medium
Character: A monospaced face designed for clarity. Taller x-height and wider apertures than most monospaced fonts — legible at the very small sizes the brand uses for labels and metadata. The technical character reinforces the quantitative positioning.

Typography Rules

Headlines use serif only. The display serif is the authority signal. Sans-serif headlines dilute the institutional tone.

Body text uses sans-serif only. Long-form reading in serif on screens reduces readability. The sans-serif body maintains clarity across all devices.

Monospaced type is always uppercase, always small. It functions as a label system — section markers, categories, metadata. Never used for paragraphs or headlines.

Copper is the accent color everywhere. Monospaced labels, section numbers, italic keywords in headlines, CTAs, and active states all use copper. The restraint of a single accent makes the brand immediately recognizable.

The Copper Rule

The single horizontal copper line between the wordmark and the descriptor is the brand's most distinctive element. It appears in the logo, as section dividers on the website, at the top of accent sections, and as a signature detail on printed materials. Always use copper (#9A6B42) — at full opacity in the logo, at reduced opacity (30–40%) when used as a page divider. The copper rule is the one element that should appear on every branded touchpoint.

"Abogados" — When to Use

The English-facing brand uses "CHAN GARCÍA" with the descriptor "Regulatory Risk & Financial Exposure Advisors · Mexico." The word "Abogados" does not appear in the international-facing brand. For the Spanish domestic site, "CHAN GARCÍA ABOGADOS" may be used with a Spanish descriptor. The two brand expressions serve different audiences and should not be mixed.

Photo Treatment

All photography should be desaturated and shifted toward the warm gray palette. High-key treatment — bright, open, with soft shadows. The goal is images that feel like they exist in the same clean, light world as the rest of the brand. No heavy contrast, no moody darkness, no saturated color.

Dark, moody imagery — the brand is light and open, not shadowy

06 — Brand character

What the brand should feel like

Chan García should feel like

A clean, well-organized assessment report

The private wealth division — precise, calm, minimal

A Swiss financial institution — clarity as a design principle

A firm that speaks the language of capital, not law

Light, open, nothing hidden — transparency as visual identity

Specialized, Mexico-focused, independent

Chan García should never feel like

A traditional law firm (no gavels, no "decades of experience")

A Big Four consultancy (no buzzwords, no frameworks-as-products)

Dark, moody, or heavy — the brand breathes through white space

A boutique anything (boutique implies small and cute)

A tech company or startup (no gradients, no rounded corners)

Promotional, sales-driven, or marketing-heavy

The visual identity should make a PE partner, a CFO, or an investment committee member feel that this firm understands their world — before they read a single word.
